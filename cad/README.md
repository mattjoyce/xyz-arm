XYZ Arm Mk1 low-poly concept
==========================

Files: `xyz-arm.scad` is the assembly; `parts/` holds reusable models; `studies/` holds reference viewers; `previews/` holds rendered images and the section SVG. Original datasheets and third-party references remain in the project root's `datasheets/` and `references/` folders.

Open [xyz-arm.scad](xyz-arm.scad) in OpenSCAD and press F5. Use the Customizer to adjust gantry height, whole-gantry extension, and phone dimensions. All dimensions are millimetres.

The assembly follows `../design.html`: 500 mm vertical and horizontal 2020 rails, four wheels with 150 mm vertical spacing, manual lock, top line guides and counterweight, flat screen-up phone, a second four-wheel plate supporting the sliding horizontal rail, and a tip-mounted light. Orange identifies concept brackets and the balance line. The A4 mat can be hidden.

This is a spatial blockout, not a printable or mechanically validated assembly. The rails now use the dimensioned OpenBuilds profile; hardware remains simplified. The 160 × 76 × 9 mm phone, base, rear counterweight shoe, guide bracket and cradle are provisional. The balance line is exaggerated to 1.1 mm diameter for visibility. Motor provisions and detailed fastener clearances are not modelled yet.

The source's side sketch puts the phone under the rail, while its thermal-pad requirement suggests contact above it. This model uses the latter: phone back on a pad above the rail, with the entire corner camera cluster projecting beyond the rail tip. Actual phone camera position and cradle clearances need measurement. The counterweight follows the carriage in the opposite direction; its guide and travel still need detailed design.

Iteration 2: the phone and light are fixed at the end of the horizontal rail. `gantry_extension` moves the whole 500 mm rail through an independent X wheel plate on the Z carriage. The rail is offset in front of the spine to allow its rear end to pass freely. X wheel stations are provisionally 96 mm apart. The camera cluster clears the tip by 8 mm; phone size and actual camera offsets still need measurement. This supersedes the independently sliding phone carriage in the original design note.

Iteration 3: the balance line now rises inside the front Z slot (negative Y), crosses the top over two provisional 4 mm smooth transverse rods, and descends in the rear slot to a counterweight lug. Two quarter turns keep the top low: the default line centre rises only 5 mm above the extrusion end. Side cheeks leave the slot exits open. `guide_diameter` adjusts the concept rods; no minimum suitable diameter or hardware has been selected. Friction, braid wear, rod finish and retention need evaluation before deciding between smooth rods and miniature pulleys.

Iteration 4: `parts/vslot-2020.scad` defines the shared rail profile from page 1 of `../datasheets/openbuilds-vslot-20x20.pdf`. Open it directly for a 60 mm sample, or enable `section_only` for its cross-section. `previews/vslot-2020-section.svg` is an exported section at nominal millimetre scale.

The profile uses the drawing's 20 mm envelope, 9.16 mm V mouth, 6.25 mm throat, 11 mm internal channel, 1.80 mm shoulder depth, 4.30 mm shoulder-to-floor depth, 90° V, 1.50 mm diagonal webs and Ø4.20 mm bore. The diagonal channel coordinates are derived from the normal web thickness, not traced by eye. Only the undimensioned outer corner radius (0.5 mm) and small central floor notches (0.2 mm) are estimated. The bore uses 64 facets independently of the assembly's low-poly setting. These are nominal dimensions, without manufacturing tolerances.

Validation: the sample exports as a closed mesh (every edge has two incident triangles), with a 20 × 20 × 60 mm bounding box. The complete assembly compiles and renders with the new shared profile.

Iteration 5: `parts/vslot-wheel.scad` replaces all eight cylindrical wheel placeholders with Solid V wheels and nominal 625 bearing pairs. Supplier reference: https://www.makerstore.com.au/product/wheel-solidv-kit/ ; drawing archived as `../datasheets/solid-v-wheel-drawing.jpg`. Uses 23.90 mm OD, 5.90 mm central tread land, 16 mm bearing seats, 14 mm central shoulder opening, and 5 mm axle bores. The kit lists 10.23 mm width whereas the drawing says 10.20 mm; the model uses the kit width. Chamfers are modelled at 45 degrees to mate with the rail faces. Bearing internals, seals, 0.23 mm central shoulder/shim and spacer shapes are simplified nominal representations.

Wheel axes now align with the rail slot centre plane. Tangency of the model's chamfers gives a wheel-axis offset of 20.32 mm from the rail centre (40.64 mm between opposing axles), with no preload. Both plates use nominal 6 mm spacers plus 1 mm external shims. Hex spacers identify the intended eccentric pair on one side of each rail; actual eccentric offsets, plate holes and hardware tolerances still need detailing. These are Solid V wheels, not grooved Dual V or Mini V wheels; confirm the purchased variant before making plates.

Iteration 6: compact carriage replaces the tall Z plate, wide X plate and connecting block. `parts/compact-carriage.scad` is a new 84 × 84 × 8 mm parametric plate, inspired by the Open Task Light body's envelope. It does not reuse that STL's hole pattern. Four wheels on each face preserve the two sliding axes. Both longitudinal wheel spacings are now 60 mm; across-rail spacing remains 40.64 mm. The 8 mm plate and nominal spacer stacks set rail centre separation to 32.23 mm. Mounting holes and two access windows are modelled; eccentric seats and lock hardware remain provisional.

At the original design note's illustrative 2.1 N·m moment, reducing Z spacing from 150 to 60 mm raises the force couple from about 14 to 35 N. This compact layout therefore needs a fresh load and settling check before fabrication. The original Open Task Light mesh remains separately available in `studies/carriage-study.scad`.

Iteration 7: three wheels per rail, six total. Each axis has two fixed wheels 60 mm apart and one central opposing wheel marked for eccentric adjustment. The shared `carriage_z_holes()` and `carriage_x_holes()` functions drive both the plate holes and assembly positions. The outline now follows the six mounting bosses and line/lock supports instead of a full square; a central access hole removes more material. The X lock placeholder is offset to clear its new central wheel. This removes two complete wheel assemblies; actual mass saving depends on purchased hardware and print settings. Eccentric seats, preload, stiffness and lock details remain unvalidated.

Iteration 8: `parts/wheel-spacer.scad` adds four concentric spacers and two eccentric spacers (gold in assembly), one per axis. The eccentric pilot axis is offset 0.395 mm from the axle, interpreting the supplier's approximate 0.79 mm adjustment range as total travel. Each adjustable plate hole follows the pilot centre, while the wheel stays at nominal tangency in the shown position. Supplier: https://www.makerstore.com.au/product/shim-eccentric-8-6/ — 6 mm standoff, 8 mm hex across flats, 10 mm flange, 5 mm bore and 7.11 mm plate hole. Collar length (1.5 mm), flange thickness (1 mm) and pilot diameter (7.08 mm) are provisional; verify against purchased parts. The flange is included within the 6 mm standoff. Hex rotation in the standalone model is an orientation parameter, not an assembled preload simulation. Nominal holes require a fit coupon before printing the final plate.

Iteration 9: removed both placeholder carriage locks, their brackets/pads and extra plate support lobes. The Olight is now fixed beneath the phone on a small magnetic disc connected to the cradle edge, with its lens pointing down. The previous outboard arm is removed. With the nominal 76 mm phone width, the 24 mm torch body sits beside the rail with 2 mm lateral clearance and stays within the phone footprint; the 28 mm disc reaches its edge. Position is toward the phone's non-camera end. Torch and mount remain approximate pending actual Olight dimensions.

Iteration 10: counterweight container is fixed at 20 mm external width across the spine. Depth extends rearward from a fixed front face at Y=14 mm, so increasing capacity does not move it toward the rail. Defaults: 60 mm depth, 90 mm height, 2 mm walls and floor; open top. `counterweight_depth` and `counterweight_height` are adjustable in the Customizer. This gives 78.85 cm³ nominal internal volume at default dimensions. Fill mass depends on material and packing; balance has not been sized from this volume. Rear shoe and line lug remain provisional and fit within the 20 mm width.

Iteration 11: `parts/z-base.scad` is the rotating base — a 140 mm plate that bolts to the turntable bearing and carries the vertical 2020. Ø140 × 51 mm, manifold, prints flat with the socket upward and needs no supports. Eight ribs at 22.5° offsets carry the plate out to the bolt circle, clearing the four mounting holes (128 mm PCD, at 0/90/180/270) and four screwdriver access holes (102 mm PCD, at 45° offsets) used to reach the other race's fixings through the plate.

Which race this bolts to is now settled: the **inner** one. A 2026-09-13 zoom on the race boundary shows countersinks on the outer ring and plain holes on the inner. A countersink seen from above takes a flat head driven downward into whatever sits below, so the outer ring is what screws to the desk and the inner ring rotates and carries this part — standard lazy-susan practice, and consistent with the brief's own fallback.

An earlier note here claimed swapping `mount_pcd` and `access_pcd` was the whole change. **That was wrong.** The underside relief has to move with the bolt circle: the plate bears on its mounting race and must clear the other, so mounting inboard means relieving *outboard*. Relieving the wrong annulus would have left the plate resting on the fixed outer ring and rubbing as it turned. `mount_race` now drives the bolt circle, the access circle and the relief annulus together, and both settings build.

A further trap caught while making that change: the derived values sat above `plate_d` in the file, and OpenSCAD resolved the forward reference to `undef`, so the relief silently evaluated inside-out. The assert caught it; the relief now keys off `bearing_od`, which is declared above and is the more meaningful bound anyway.

No central screw into the rail's tapped Ø4.2 end bore. Its head counterbore (Ø9.5, reaching y = −4.75) collides with the rear slot channel the balance line runs down (y = −3.90 to −8.20 from the profile drawing); the two cannot share the socket floor. The rail is instead held by four M5 bolts into drop-in tee nuts in both side slots at two heights (14 and 34 mm above the plate), which resists the tipping moment as a force couple rather than as clamp friction on a plastic floor. **Slide the tee nuts in before seating the rail** — its lower end is captive in the socket.

The balance line runs straight down inside the rail's rear slot and through a Ø4 hole at y = −6.05, asserted to stay clear of both the slot floor and the shoulder. Keeping it in the slot rather than outside the rail also keeps it near the rotation axis, so a ±45° sweep moves it about 4.6 mm rather than 13.8 mm — which sets how big the hole through the desk needs to be.

Load check: at 2.18 N·m the socket's square section (32.4 mm outside, 20.8 inside, S = 4929 mm³) sees **0.44 MPa**, roughly a hundredth of PETG's yield. Strength is not the question here; stiffness and long-term creep are, which is what the ribs are for. Print with generous wall count rather than relying on infill. Tee bolts see about 109 N each, trivial for M5.

Not yet resolved: the bearing's bore, race boundary and hole pitch circles remain photo estimates from `studies/turntable-bearing.md`, and every one of them feeds this part's geometry. Measure them before printing a final part. The asserts will catch a combination that cannot be built, but they cannot catch numbers that are merely wrong.

Wheel spacing stays compact at 60 mm by explicit decision, accepting about 40 N per wheel pair at the corrected 2.4 N·m rather than 16 N at 150 mm. To be revisited only if it proves to settle too slowly in use.

Iteration 12: the assembly adopts the real base. `frame()` drops the 180 × 160 blockout slab and its accent cone for `turntable_bearing()` on the desk, `z_base()` bolted to it, and the rail seated in the socket floor. The Z datum is now explicitly the **desk surface**: bearing 0–8.5, plate 8.5–14.5, rail seated at 16.5. `base_h` is computed from `turntable_thickness()` and `zbase_rail_seat()` rather than the old hardcoded 12, so changing either part moves the assembly with it. Both parts gained accessor functions for this; `use` imports modules and functions but not top-level variables, so the assembly could not otherwise see their internals.

The counterweight leaves the spine. It was a 20 mm slab riding alongside in the rear slot; it is now a plain pot hanging below the desk on the line, because the desk-mounted build removes the envelope that forced the slab shape. Defaults are Ø60 × 80 mm — about 222 cm³, which is 1.02 kg of steel shot at bulk 4.6 g/cm³, or the same mass in lead at Ø60 × 53. `counterweight_diameter` and `counterweight_height` replace the old depth/height pair. Travel mirrors the carriage: the pot starts 20 mm below the desk and descends as the gantry rises.

The balance line's rear run now goes straight down the rear slot, through the base's Ø4 hole at y = +6.05, and through the desk. `z-base.scad`'s `line_y` moved from the front slot to the rear to match — the sign only selects which slot, and the assert on the channel bounds holds either way. A new `show_desk` draws an 18 mm slab with the 25 mm pass-through, sized for the line's ±45° arc.

The mat also moved. It was drawn at z = −3.5 to −0.5, i.e. floating below the desk surface it is meant to sit on; it now occupies 0–3 with its grid on top.

Clearance check at the bottom of travel: the socket's top lands at z = 59.5 and the carriage's lower edge at `gantry_height` 110 reaches z = 68, leaving **8.5 mm**. The camera then sits about 117 mm above the desk, so roughly 114 mm above the mat's top face — inside the 98 mm close-focus limit with margin, and at the bottom of the 100–300 mm useful band. The socket depth therefore sets the bottom of the working range, and shortening it would buy back travel that the optics cannot use anyway.

Iteration 13: bought and context geometry now carries the `%` background modifier — rails, wheels, spacers, axles, guide rods, the bearing, the balance line, phone, Olight body, mat and desk. They still draw for reference but are excluded from render and export, so what survives an F6 is exactly the set of printed parts: 7050 facets, with the desk and mat gone from the bounding box. This replaced an earlier attempt to back the wheels off the rail by 0.02 mm, which was hacking around a display artefact rather than stating the intent.

`rod()` gained an epsilon extension at both ends. The balance line is drawn as a chain of segments that previously butted end-to-end, sharing an exact coplanar face — the classic way to make a union non-manifold. The counterweight lug and the mat grid lines had the same problem against the surfaces they sat on, and were sunk slightly into them.

On the 2-manifold warning, what was actually established rather than assumed: `frame()` and `moving_arm()` each render clean **alone**, and so does every part in `parts/`. The assembly does not. Toggling the mat, desk and phone off changes nothing, so the cause is not the context geometry. The wheels are placed at nominal tangency to the rail — Iteration 5's note says so explicitly — and two solids touching along a line are neither disjoint nor overlapping, which is exactly the degenerate case. `%` disposes of that for the export.

A warning still remains among the printed placeholder brackets (cradle, lugs, light mount), and is **not** resolved. It is not worth more CGAL renders: parts are printed individually, each one is clean on its own, and the assembly exists to check spatial fit rather than to be exported as a body. Worth revisiting only if those brackets stop being placeholders.

Individual parts are checked separately — `z-base.scad` renders `Simple: yes` with 1248 facets, and all four tee-bolt holes were verified present in its mesh rather than by eye, since the render angle hides the upper pair.

Iteration 14: `parts/base-fit-test.scad` is a Ø145 × 2 mm gauge that answers both open base questions in one short print, rather than committing to the Ø140 × 51 mm part.

Three 2020 apertures at 0.15 / 0.30 / 0.45 mm per side span wide on purpose; narrow the bracket on the next print once this says which end is right. Corner reliefs are included because FDM cannot cut a sharp internal corner — without them the nozzle's corner bulge carries the fit and the flats never seat, so the reading would be corner interference rather than clearance. Both candidate bolt circles are on the rim, so the 102 PCD can be confirmed while 128 stays available as a cross-check on the photo estimate.

Marking is tally holes, not text: round holes print at travel speed where small glyph perimeters do not. They sit below their aperture, which keys the orientation — read them above and the plate is flipped, and the clearance order would otherwise reverse.

Four hull cutouts remove 36 % of the disc, against 12 % for an earlier field of twenty small perforations. Each is verified by `studies/check-cutouts.py`, which measures point-to-segment distance from every cutout to the aperture zone, all eight bolt holes and the rim. Two of the four failed first time. The instructive one was the top cutout: its vertices cleared the diagonal bolt at 12.7 mm while the **edge between two vertices** passed within 5.32 mm — invisible in plan view, and the reason this is checked numerically. Worst case as built is zone 4.50, bolt 7.00, rim 7.55 against needs of 4.0 / 6.6 / 3.5, with left and right deliberately backed 0.5 mm off their exact limits so polygon tessellation cannot eat the margin. Re-run the script after changing any cutout number.

Lightening stops there on purpose. A gauge earns its keep by being a rigid reference; perforate 2 mm further and it flexes, and a flexing plate can be forced onto a bolt pattern that does not line up or deformed around a rail that is really too tight — a false pass on both tests at once.

What the print cannot settle: a 2 mm aperture reads XY clearance almost purely, whereas the real socket is 45 mm deep where wall lean and friction both bite, so the winning clearance will feel tighter at full depth. And the number belongs to the printer, nozzle, material, layer height and XY compensation, not to the geometry.

Iteration 15: the base's eight ribs are gone. A truncated cone now runs from the plate top to the socket mouth: Ø88 at the foot, flat top flush with the top of the socket at 45 mm, Ø49.1 there (the boss's 44.1 mm corner diagonal + 5), so the flank rises at about 67°. The square boss is swallowed whole and the rail rises out of a plain round face. It narrows upward, so it still prints without supports. Asserts keep the foot 2 mm inside the mount screw pilots (whose tips come up through the plate) and the screwdriver access holes. Both tee bolt pairs, at 14 and 34 mm, now sit inside the cone, so their Ø9.5 head bores run straight out through it (about 13 mm deep), and a hex key reaches them from outside. The cone's derived sizes are declared after `boss`, because of the forward-reference trap from Iteration 11.

Socket clearance is 0.375 mm per side, halfway between the gauge's two workable apertures: 0.45 slid on easily, 0.30 went on hard at only 2 mm deep. Mount holes are Ø3.5 pilots for M4 self-tapping screws driven up through the bearing's inner ring. The float lives in the bearing's own loose holes. The rail hold-down is M5 × 8 socket heads into drop-in tee nuts; 10 mm would bottom out in the slot channel. Previews: `previews/z-base.png`, `z-base-section.png`, `z-base-context.png`.

The plate no longer covers the outer race. Mounted on the inner race, it is Ø112: the race boundary estimate (114) less 1 mm radial gap. The access holes and underside relief are gone, and the outer race's countersunk desk screws are simply in the open. That leaves 3.25 mm of plastic outboard of each Ø3.5 pilot on the 102 circle, asserted ≥ 3. The boundary is still a photo estimate, and if the real one is smaller the rim will rub as it turns. `mount_race = "outer"` still builds the full Ø140 plate with access holes and relief.

Edges: the plate and cone are now one revolved profile (the square boss was wholly inside the cone, so it is no longer modelled). Rim chamfers are 1.0 mm top and 0.5 mm bottom; the bottom one also clears elephant's foot. The cone foot has a 4 mm fillet, where the tipping moment concentrates, and its top edge a 2 mm round. The socket mouth has a 1 mm lead-in chamfer and each M4 pilot a 0.5 mm one on the underside. `fillet_pts()` builds both the fillet and the round from the corner and its two edge directions. The four tee bolt bores are left sharp, since they meet the curved flank.

Race boundary taken as 113 (1 mm under the 114 photo estimate), so the plate is Ø111. That cuts the plastic outboard of each pilot to 2.75 mm, and `mount_edge_min` is relaxed to 2.5 to allow it. With four perimeters that is nearly solid wall.

Iteration 16: `z-base.scad` gains `variant = "full" | "fast"`. Full is unchanged: its re-exported STL has the identical vertex set, and it is still `parts/z-base.stl`. Fast is `parts/z-base-fast.stl`, exported with `-D 'variant="fast"'`. It has the same bearing pilots, socket, fit, line hole and tee bolts, at about a third of the volume (70 cm³ against 204).

- **Plate:** a Ø70 hub with four 12 mm arms out to round screw lugs, replacing the Ø111 disc. The lugs reach the same Ø111 rim, so the pilot edge is still 2.75 mm. The arm roots are filleted.
- **Socket:** stands as an open square boss on a 12 mm flared, filleted foot instead of being buried in the cone. The tee bolts rise to 20 and 38 mm, so their counterbores sit on the open boss face above the foot. M5 × 8 still applies, and there are no bores through a flank.
- **Check:** arm bending at the hub is about 6 MPa at 21 N per screw. That is fine for strength, but it is the softest part of this variant.

Context previews now turn the bearing's free inner ring so its holes sit under the pilots.

Tee screw length, corrected. The earlier "M5 × 8, 2.8 mm under the head" was wrong for the model as built. The counterbore starts 1 mm proud of the boss face, so a 3 mm sink left 3.8 mm of wall, and an M5 × 8 would reach only 2.0 mm past the slot lip. The sink is now 4 mm (2.8 mm of wall), which gives 3.0 mm into the tee nut. Asserts now check the stack: M5 × 6 fails as too short, and M5 × 10 fails because it bottoms in the 4.3 mm channel. Both STLs are re-exported.

Iteration 17: the tee bolt holes become nut windows. The rail is held by drop-in tee nuts in its side slots, and once it is seated in the socket the only way into a slot is through the socket wall, so a Ø5.5 bolt bore is no use: the nut has to pass through the wall first. Each station is now a rectangular window, `tee_nut_w + 2·clr` across the slot by `tee_nut_l + 2·clr` along it — 6.8 × 10.8 for the standard 20-series M5 nut (10 × 6 × 4) at 0.4 mm clearance — with 0.5 mm corner radii. The nut goes in long side up the slot, drops through the 6.25 mm throat, and the screw turns it home in the channel, as for any blind drop-in. Both variants share `tee_windows()`.

The head no longer has a full seat: the M5 socket head is Ø8.5 and the window 6.8 wide, so it bears on two 0.85 mm strips beside the window (asserted ≥ 0.75). Screw stack at that point: 3 mm counterbore into the wall, M5 × 8, 3.0 mm into the nut (changed in iteration 19). If the seat crushes, a button-head or flanged M5 spreads it. In the full variant the flank bore grows from Ø9.5 to Ø12 (`tee_access`, asserted ≥ window height + 1) so the nut can pass it on the way to the window; the fast variant keeps the Ø9.5 counterbore, which the window's ends run 0.65 mm past on the open boss face. Top-of-socket and foot clearances now use the larger of head and window. Volumes: full 200 cm³, fast 69. Preview: `previews/z-base-fast-window.png`.

Note for headless work: the revolved body of the full variant does not draw in OpenCSG `--preview` from the command line, only its cutters do. `--render` is fine and all previews are made that way.

Iteration 18: `variant = "fins"`, exported to `parts/z-base-fins.stl`. It keeps the fast variant's hub-and-arms plate and its bare square boss, and takes three things out. The flared foot is replaced by four gusset fins, 2.4 mm thick and 30 mm up the boss, each running from a boss corner out along an arm to 44 mm, short of the lug (asserted). The socket floor is cut out, so the pocket runs through the plate and the rail is located by its tee bolts alone; it can drop on through the bearing bore to whatever is under it, and `zbase_rail_seat()` now returns 0 for this variant so the assembly places it at the plate underside. The boss is turned 45° about Z (`boss_turn`), which is what lets the fins root at the corners and leave the nut windows on the faces alone; the rail's slots, and with them the tee windows and the balance line's rear slot, therefore point at 45°, 135°, 225° and 315° on this base, not down the arms. The lower tee pair goes back to 14 mm, since nothing is in the way. No line hole is cut: the rear slot is open to the underside through the pocket. Fast and fins share `boss_body()`, `tee_counterbores()` and `rail_pocket(through=)`. Volume 53 cm³ against 69 for fast. Previews: `previews/z-base-fins.png`, `z-base-fins-under.png`.

Iteration 19: tee screws are M5 × 10, the length on hand, instead of × 8. The head counterbore now stops 1 mm into the wall rather than 3 (`tee_head_sink` 2, from 1 mm proud of the boss face), so 4.8 mm of wall sits under the head and a 10 reaches 3.0 mm into the nut, the same engagement the 8 had with the deeper sink. The asserts still bracket it: 2.5 mm minimum in the nut, 3.5 maximum before the screw bottoms in the 4.3 mm slot channel. The head stands about 4 mm proud of the boss face in fast and fins, which nothing obstructs; in full it is reached through the same Ø12 flank bore. All three STLs re-exported.

Head diameter measured at 9.26 mm, which is an M5 button head, not the 8.5 mm cap head assumed so far. `tee_head_d` is now that measurement and the counterbore `tee_head` is derived from it, 0.6 mm a side, so Ø10.46. The wider head helps the nut window: the seat strips either side of it grow from 0.85 to 1.23 mm. In full the Ø12 flank bore already clears it. All three STLs re-exported.

Tee nut measured at 9.85 × 5.9 mm, replacing the nominal 10 × 6. The windows follow: 6.7 across the slot by 10.65 along it at the same 0.4 mm clearance, and the head seat strips are 1.28 mm. All three STLs re-exported.

Iteration 20: the tee bolt entry is a switch, `tee_entry = auto | window | round`. A drop-in nut sits wholly inside the slot, so nuts can be loaded before the rail goes in and found blind through a plain Ø5.5 hole; the window only adds fitting a nut with the rail clamped. Auto gives windows where the rail is captive (full, fast) and round holes where it slides through (fins), so on fins the 9.26 mm head gets a full ring seat again and the boss face keeps more of its wall. `tee_holes()` cuts either. Nut loading on fins: bolt heights are 14 and 34 mm above the plate top, so with the rail standing on the desk through the bearing bore the nuts sit about 28.5 and 48.5 mm up from its bottom end. `z-base-fins.stl` re-exported; preview `previews/z-base-fins-holes.png`.

`parts/socket-test.scad` is the top 20 mm of the fins variant's boss on its own (`test_h`), keeping the mouth lead-in: 32.35 mm square, through pocket at the 0.375 mm/side fit, one bolt station, the base's upper one, 9 mm up the piece, with the round Ø5.5 holes and Ø10.46 counterbores, and nothing else. One station tests everything the holes need testing; `tee_holes()` and `tee_counterbores()` take a heights list for it. It is built from `z-base.scad`'s own `boss_body()`, `rail_pocket()`, `tee_holes()` and `tee_counterbores()` under `use`, so it is the same geometry as the base and a pass on it is a pass on the base. 12 cm³. Exported to `parts/socket-test.stl`, preview `previews/socket-test.png`.

Iteration 21: keyhole entry for the tee nuts, after the socket test. Round holes printed and screwed up fine, but loading nuts blind into a 500 mm rail is a fight, and a plain window costs the head its seat. `tee_entry = "keyhole"` puts the nut window above the hole and joins the two with a slot the width of the screw shank: start the screw two turns into the nut, feed the nut through the window into the rail slot, slide the screw down until the head drops into its counterbore, tighten. The nut rides captive on the screw and the head seats on a ring open only on the slot side. The window sits just clear of the counterbore (`keyhole_rise` = 10.56), so it needs 15.9 mm of wall above each hole and two stations do not fit a 45 mm socket: fins goes to a single pair at 27 mm, its window topping out 2.1 mm under the mouth. If one pair lets the rail rock, `socket_h = 55` makes room for two again. Auto now gives keyhole on fins, windows on full and fast; the asserts catch a keyhole into the mouth or into the next station's counterbore, and one into a buried boss. `z-base-fins.stl` re-exported (53 cm³). `socket-test.scad` follows: top 25 mm of the socket, the keyholed station 7 mm up, 14 cm³.

Iteration 22: the two fins stations share one keyhole window. The socket stays 45 mm. The window sits midway between the stations, at 23 mm, and a shank-wide slot runs from it down to the lower hole and up to the upper one: each screw goes in with its nut at the window and slides to its own counterbore, one down, one up. Stations are 10 and 36 mm, a 26 mm couple, with 4.8 mm of wall under the lower counterbore and 2.8 above the upper. `keyhole_dir = drop | lift | shared` picks the layout; auto is shared on fins. `keyhole_mid` is declared after `tee_heights` on purpose, because the first cut of this had it before and the forward reference came back undef: every assert passed, both meshes rendered `Simple: yes`, and neither had a keyhole in it. Check the previews, not just the asserts. `z-base-fins.stl` re-exported, 52 cm³. `socket-test.scad` is now the whole 45 mm socket with both stations, since the shared window spans them, 25 cm³.

Iteration 23: three changes to the fins socket.
